## Subjective

Patient is a 45-year-old female presenting with a two-week history of increased thirst, frequent
urination (approximately 8–10 times per day including two nocturnal episodes), and fatigue.
She states she has been "a bit diabetic" according to a relative who checked her blood sugar at
home with a personal glucometer, which apparently read "very high" — but she does not remember
the exact number and does not have the device with her. She has never been formally diagnosed
with diabetes and has not been seen by a physician for about four years.

She denies fever, nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain. She reports no chest pain or shortness of
breath. She has had some blurring of vision over the past few weeks, particularly in the right eye,
which she attributes to "tiredness." No headache.

Medications: None prescribed. She takes "some supplement from the health food store" for energy
but cannot recall the name. Occasional ibuprofen for back pain.

Allergies: None known.

Social history: Works as a bus driver. Smokes approximately 10 cigarettes per day for 15 years.
Drinks alcohol on weekends (self-reports "a few beers"). No recreational drugs. Lives with her
two adult children.

Family history: Mother has "sugar" (diabetes, type unknown to patient). Maternal uncle had a
foot amputation — cause unknown to patient.

## Assessment

1. **Diabetes mellitus, type unspecified** — patient presents with classic hyperglycaemic
   symptoms and in-clinic glucose of 18.2 mmol/L. She has not been formally diagnosed
   previously and type (1 vs. 2) has not been determined. Given age, BMI, and family history,
   Type 2 is suspected but cannot be confirmed without further workup including fasting glucose,
   HbA1c, and possible C-peptide/antibody testing.

2. **Hypertension** — BP elevated at 148/92 on two readings today. May be related to
   uncontrolled diabetes or may be independent. No prior diagnosis on record.

3. **Obesity** — BMI 35.8. Likely contributing to insulin resistance.

4. **Visual disturbance, right eye** — possibly related to hyperglycaemia/diabetic retinopathy;
   cannot exclude refractive change or other cause. Requires formal ophthalmology referral.

5. **Smoking** — active smoker, 10 cigarettes/day × 15 years; heightened cardiovascular risk
   in setting of possible diabetes and hypertension.

---

## Plan

- Urgent labs ordered: fasting glucose, HbA1c, full metabolic panel (including renal function),
  lipid panel, thyroid function, urinalysis with urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio
- Start metformin 500 mg once daily with food pending lab confirmation, with plan to titrate
- Refer to ophthalmology urgently for formal retinal examination
- Smoking cessation counselling provided; offered nicotine replacement therapy
- Return in 1 week for lab review and blood pressure follow-up
- Patient instructed to go to emergency department if she develops vomiting, confusion,
  or shortness of breath before next appointment

**Note:** Diabetes type, current HbA1c, and organ involvement remain undetermined pending labs.
ICD-10 coding will require clarification at next visit.
