## Subjective

The patient is a 32-year-old male presenting for his annual well-man examination. He has no
active complaints today. He reports feeling generally well and performing well at work. He
exercises three to four times per week (gym and occasional running). He denies any new symptoms.

He mentions that he "takes levothyroxine every morning" and has done so for "a few years," but
is uncertain of the dose. He cannot remember why it was started or who prescribed it. He does
not have prior medical records with him. He reports no symptoms of hypothyroidism (no fatigue,
cold intolerance, hair loss, constipation) or hyperthyroidism (no palpitations, heat intolerance,
weight loss, tremor).

He also takes a daily multivitamin and protein powder.

He reports occasional heartburn after large meals, approximately twice a month, self-managed
with over-the-counter omeprazole 20 mg, which he purchases himself without a prescription.
He considers this a minor issue and does not believe it warrants attention.

Allergies: Sulfonamides (hives).

Social history: Non-smoker; drinks alcohol socially (4–6 units per week). Works in software
engineering. Single, no children. No recreational drug use.

Family history: Father has rheumatoid arthritis. Mother is healthy. Younger sister was recently
diagnosed with "some thyroid problem" (details unknown to patient).

## Assessment

1. **Annual preventive examination** — patient is an otherwise-healthy 32-year-old male with
   no current active complaints. Well-appearing; BMI, BP, and HR within normal limits.

2. **Levothyroxine use — underlying diagnosis not documented** — patient is taking levothyroxine
   but is unaware of the original diagnosis. The most likely underlying condition is hypothyroidism
   (possibly Hashimoto's thyroiditis given the family history), but this cannot be confirmed or
   coded without thyroid function test results. No prior thyroid diagnosis is available in this
   visit's documentation.

3. **Self-managed gastro-oesophageal reflux symptoms** — patient reports intermittent heartburn
   managed with self-purchased omeprazole. No formal diagnosis of GORD/GERD documented.
   Symptoms appear mild and infrequent.

4. **Family history of rheumatoid arthritis** — noted; no current joint symptoms; patient has
   no examination findings to suggest inflammatory arthropathy.

---

## Plan

- Order thyroid function panel: TSH, free T4 — to determine underlying diagnosis for levothyroxine
  use and confirm appropriate dosing
- Request release of prior medical records from any previous providers
- Recommend patient continue levothyroxine until thyroid results reviewed at follow-up
- Discuss ongoing heartburn: if symptoms persist more than twice weekly or worsen, formal
  evaluation recommended (endoscopy referral)
- Age-appropriate screening: blood pressure monitoring, cholesterol check (last unknown),
  testicular self-examination counselling provided
- Return in 2 weeks for thyroid results review or sooner if new symptoms

**Note to coder:** Levothyroxine is listed in patient's self-reported medication history, but
NO confirmed diagnosis of hypothyroidism or any thyroid disorder is documented in this visit.
The underlying indication is not established and should not be assumed for ICD-10 coding
purposes at this time.
